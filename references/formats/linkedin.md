**Length:** 150–400 words for standard posts. Educational breakdowns can run to 500 if the structure justifies it.

**Line breaks:** Use them deliberately. Space equals clarity on LinkedIn. Short paragraphs. One idea per block. Let the reader breathe between points.

**Opening:** Lead with a pattern, a tension, a data point, or a specific moment. See Hook Rules below for what's allowed and what's banned.

**Closing:** Depends on post type:
- **Insight/POV/Signal/Commentary posts:** End with a reframe.
- **Educational posts:** End with a "what's next" or series trail.
- **Community/milestone posts:** End with "Onwards!" or a forward-looking statement.
- **Any post type:** Can close with tension or a challenge instead. See voice-profile.template.md for closing guidance by register.

**Formatting by post type:**
- Types A–D: Prose only. No headers, no bullet lists, no emoji markers.
- Type E (Educational): Emoji markers (🔹, ➡️) or Unicode bold headers (𝗕𝗼𝗹𝗱 𝘁𝗲𝘅𝘁) for sections. Structure serves the content.
- Type F (Community): Conversational. Tags. A few emojis naturally.
- Never mix prose and structured formatting in one post.

## The 5-Part Architecture

This is the universal rhythm underneath all of [YOUR FOUNDER/CEO]'s post types. Individual types (A–F) are variations of this architecture. When in doubt about how to structure a post, return to this.

```
1. HOOK / TRIGGER
   Challenge a false belief or highlight a hidden problem.
   "Most innovation fails because there's no system to deliver it."

2. REAL INSIGHT / STORY
   From a customer, a vendor, a boardroom conversation. Grounded in experience.
   "We saw a top organisation shift from 6-month onboarding to 2 weeks..."

3. REFRAME THE PROBLEM
   Teach a new way to see the issue. This is the turn.
   "Innovation isn't a strategy issue. It's a supply chain one."

4. PRINCIPLE / FRAMEWORK
   Something memorable, useful, and repeatable.
   "Demo-to-production is the new conversion metric."

5. CLOSE WITH TENSION / CHALLENGE
   A line that provokes or reframes. See 07 for closing register.
   "If you're still buying demos, you're not buying outcomes."
```

Not every post hits all five. Types A and D often compress 3–5 into a single reframe. Type C spends more time in step 2. Type E distributes step 2 across structured sections. But the underlying rhythm is always: hook → evidence → reframe → principle → tension.

**Emojis:** Max 2–3 per post. Never as the opening character. Never decorative. Each one earns its place. See voice-profile.template.md for permitted emojis and their usage.

**Tagging:** Tag collaborators, co-panellists, and team members when relevant.

**Hashtags:** 2–4 at the very end. Industry-relevant (#[industry-specific], #[your industry], #AI), never trending-chasing. Event-specific hashtags for event posts.

## Hook Rules

The hook is the first 1–2 lines. A cheap hook that gets clicks and loses respect is worse than no hook at all.

### Banned Hooks

| Pattern | Example |
|---------|---------|
| Dangling rhetorical question | "What if your organisation could onboard [industry-specific]s in days?" |
| Vague provocation | "Nobody is talking about this." |
| Hyperbolic claim | "This will change everything about AI adoption." |
| Listicle tease | "3 things I learned about vendor onboarding." |
| Fake vulnerability | "I'll be honest, I almost gave up on enterprise AI." |
| "Hot take:" / "Unpopular opinion:" | "Hot take: procurement is the real bottleneck." |
| "Most people think X" | "Most people think AI adoption is about models." |
| Generic excitement | "Exciting news! We are thrilled to announce..." |
| AI-generated openers | "In today's rapidly evolving landscape..." |
| Fear-based clickbait | "If you're not doing this, you're already dead." |

### Approved Hooks

**1. The Pattern Statement** — Something the reader has seen but hasn't named yet.
> "Every enterprise AI programme we see on the platform hits the same wall at month four."

**2. The Data Anchor** — A specific number, stat, or observation.
> "78% of AI vendor evaluations take longer than the project they're evaluating for."

**3. The Tension Opener** — A contradiction the reader lives with daily.
> "Boards want AI speed. Compliance wants AI caution. Nobody has built the bridge between them."

**4. The Scene Drop** — A specific, recognisable moment. Anonymised.
> "Last week I sat in a room where a CTO showed a board deck with 14 AI pilots. None were in production."

**5. The Quiet Observation** — Short, declarative. No drama.
> "Vendor onboarding is where AI ambition goes to die."

**6. The Achievement Lead** — For milestone/community posts only. State what happened.
> "[YOUR COMPANY] recognised as one of the top 10 fastest growing [industry-specific]s according to [YOUR INDUSTRY RANKING]."

### Hook Self-Test

1. **Could any LinkedIn thought leader have written this?** If yes, too generic. Rewrite.
2. **Does it contain a specific detail?** A number, a timeframe, a scenario.
3. **Would [YOUR FOUNDER] actually say this in a meeting?** If it sounds like copywriting, it's wrong.

## Type A — Insight / Point-of-View Post

**Purpose:** Name the structural reality of enterprise AI adoption before anyone else.

**Format:** Pure prose.

```
HOOK
  Pattern or tension the reader instantly recognises.
  Grounded in something specific.

BUILD
  Unpack the insight. Evidence first, opinion follows.
  Reference platform data where natural.
  Make the "so what" explicit.

REFRAME
  Flip the conventional wisdom.
```

**When to use:** Enterprise AI trends, vendor/buyer dynamics, procurement failures, structural arguments about adoption speed.

---

## Type B — Signal / Data Post

**Purpose:** Ground an argument in anonymised platform data or public reports.

**Format:** Prose-first. Can set apart one or two data callouts for emphasis.

```
DATA HOOK
  Lead with the number, stat, or observation.

CONTEXT
  Why this matters. What it reveals that isn't obvious.
  Connect to a broader structural argument.

IMPLICATION
  What this means for the reader's organisation.

REFRAME
```

**When to use:** After earnings calls, analyst reports, regulatory announcements, platform data patterns.

---

## Type C — Narrative / Story Post

**Purpose:** Ground an insight in a real (anonymised) scenario.

**Format:** Pure prose.

```
SCENE SET
  Drop the reader into a situation. Specific enough to feel real,
  anonymised enough to protect identity.

TENSION
  What went wrong, or what the gap was.
  The structural problem, not just the symptom.

RESOLUTION / LESSON
  What changed. What the insight is.

REFRAME
```

**When to use:** Customer conversations, deal patterns, scenarios that illustrate a broader truth.

---

## Type D — Industry Commentary / React Post

**Purpose:** React to news, an article, or an industry event with a sharper read.

**Format:** Prose.

```
ANCHOR
  Reference the news/article/event briefly. Don't summarise it.

CEO'S READ
  What everyone else is missing. The structural angle.
  Evidence-first.

REFRAME
  The line that repositions the conversation.
```

**When to use:** Breaking [industry-specific]/AI news, competitor announcements, regulatory changes, viral industry posts.

---

## Type E — Educational / Breakdown Post

**Purpose:** Break down a complex industry development for the reader.

**Format:** Structured. Emoji markers (🔹, ➡️) and Unicode bold headers are appropriate here.

```
CONTEXT HOOK
  What's changing and why it matters. No throat-clearing.

BREAKDOWN (structured sections)
  🔹 or ➡️ markers for each key point.
  Each point: what it is + why it matters. Keep each section tight.

SO WHAT / WHAT'S NEXT
  Practical implication.
  Can trail: "I'll be exploring this more in further posts this week."
```

**When to use:** Regulatory changes ([YOUR RELEVANT REGULATIONS]), technology migrations, standards shifts, anything needing structured breakdown.

---

## Type F — Community / Milestone / Event Recap Post

**Purpose:** Celebrate achievements, recap events, build the ecosystem.

**Format:** Conversational. Tags people. A few emojis. Ends with "Onwards!" or forward look.

```
WHAT HAPPENED
  Lead with the achievement or event. Be specific.
  Don't open with "Exciting news!" — state the news itself.

CONTEXT / COLOUR
  What made it meaningful. Who was there. What stood out.
  Tag collaborators and relevant people.

FORWARD LOOK
  What this means for what's next.
```

**When to use:** After events ([YOUR INDUSTRY EVENTS]), milestones ([YOUR MILESTONES — e.g. rankings, partnerships, fundraising]), panel appearances, ecosystem wins.

**Rules specific to this type:**
- Lead with the achievement, not with how you feel about it
- Tag people generously but specifically
- Photo or visual if available
- 3–5 hashtags including event-specific ones
