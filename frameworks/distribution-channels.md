# F6: Distribution Channels

Framework for `distribution_channels` table. Where content is published. Consumed by Content Distributor (routing decisions) and Ghost Writer (channel-aware tone and format).

Each channel defines: what it is, what content belongs there, what content types (see F5a) it accepts, and channel-specific notes.

Audience is defined in F1 (ICP Profiles). This framework does not duplicate it.

This framework will scale as new channels are added.

---

## LinkedIn — [YOUR COMPANY] Page

**channel_slug:** `linkedin-[your-company]`

What it is: [YOUR COMPANY]'s company page on LinkedIn. The brand channel. Corporate voice with personality.

What belongs here: Content from your content pillars (see F3). Topics aligned with your company's authority areas and product positioning.

Content types accepted: Short-form text post, long-form text post, carousel, infographic, one-pager.

Channel-specific notes: Company pages receive lower organic reach than personal profiles. Employee reshares and paid amplification close the gap. Posts from this channel should be designed for reshareability by team members.

---

## LinkedIn — [YOUR FOUNDER/CEO]'s Page

**channel_slug:** `[your-personal-channel]`

What it is: [YOUR FOUNDER/CEO]'s personal LinkedIn profile. The founder/CEO voice. Authority, perspective, direct engagement.

What belongs here: Content from your content pillars (see F3). Thought leadership, market commentary, social proof, personal insights, and company perspective.

Content types accepted: Short-form text post, long-form text post, carousel, infographic, one-pager.

Channel-specific notes: Higher organic reach than the [YOUR COMPANY] company page. Expertise signals and conversational engagement drive distribution. Pre-post and post-publication engagement (see F4a) apply here. This is the primary organic reach channel.

## [YOUR PUBLICATION]

**channel_slug:** `[your-publication]`

What it is: Published articles in [YOUR PUBLICATION]. Third-party credibility at scale. [YOUR FOUNDER] writes as a contributor — the content carries [YOUR PUBLICATION]' editorial weight.

What belongs here: Long-form thought leadership. Enterprise AI adoption, agentic AI, responsible innovation, ecosystem-level trends, governance. Content must stand on its own as a [YOUR PUBLICATION] article — no overt [YOUR COMPANY] product content. [YOUR COMPANY] can be referenced as context or evidence but the article is not about [YOUR COMPANY].

Content types accepted: Blog post / article ([YOUR PUBLICATION] format — typically 800–1,500 words).

Channel-specific notes: [YOUR PUBLICATION] articles have long-tail SEO value — they rank in search and surface for months or years. A single [YOUR PUBLICATION] article can generate multiple LinkedIn posts (key insights extracted as short-form text posts, carousels, or infographics). [YOUR PUBLICATION] publication is a social proof event in itself — the fact of publication can be posted on LinkedIn under KP-03 Social Proof.

## [YOUR PARTNER]

**channel_slug:** `[your-partner]`

What it is: Content published through or in collaboration with [YOUR PARTNER]'s channels. Co-branded or guest content within [YOUR PARTNER]'s ecosystem — blog, events, reports, partner spotlights.

What belongs here: Content at the intersection of [YOUR COMPANY]'s capabilities and [YOUR PARTNER]'s ecosystem. [Evaluation Environment] experimentation, vendor evaluation, AI adoption within [YOUR PARTNER]-connected institutions, partnership outcomes, joint case studies.

Content types accepted: Blog post / article, case study (packaged as article), white paper (co-branded), one-pager.

Channel-specific notes: Content must be relevant to [YOUR PARTNER]'s ecosystem, not just [YOUR COMPANY]'s. The framing is "what [YOUR PARTNER] customers can do" not "what [YOUR COMPANY] does." Joint branding and approval processes apply. Content published here can be repurposed on LinkedIn with appropriate attribution.

## [YOUR NEWSLETTER] Newsletter

**channel_slug:** `[your-newsletter]`

What it is: [YOUR COMPANY]'s holistic newsletter targeting CFOs. Direct-to-inbox. Owned distribution.

What belongs here: Content framed through a CFO lens. AI investment decisions, cost of experimentation vs. cost of inaction, ROI evidence, risk reduction, regulatory compliance implications, budget justification, vendor evaluation economics, build vs. buy financial analysis. Content from multiple pillars can feed [YOUR NEWSLETTER] but must be reframed for the CFO audience — the same case study that appears as a technical evaluation on LinkedIn appears as a cost-saving evidence point in [YOUR NEWSLETTER].

Content types accepted: Email newsletter (the primary format — curated, concise, professionally framed), with links to blog posts / articles, case studies, white papers, and one-pagers for deeper reading.

Channel-specific notes: Bypasses all platform algorithms — delivery is guaranteed to subscribers. Open rates and click-through rates are the primary metrics. Cadence, tone, and framing are distinct from LinkedIn. Every piece of content in [YOUR NEWSLETTER] should answer: "Why should a CFO care about this?"

## [YOUR NEWSLETTER PLATFORM]

**channel_slug:** `[your-newsletter-platform]`

What it is: [YOUR FOUNDER/CEO]'s [YOUR NEWSLETTER PLATFORM] publication. Long-form, serialised thought leadership. Deeper, more considered than LinkedIn posts. Builds a subscriber base outside of LinkedIn's algorithm.

What belongs here: Long-form thought leadership that goes deeper than LinkedIn allows. Enterprise AI adoption essays, detailed frameworks, extended case study analysis, multi-part series, annual reflections, strategic predictions. Content that would be too long or too detailed for a LinkedIn post but too opinionated or personal for [YOUR PUBLICATION].

Content types accepted: Blog post / article ([YOUR NEWSLETTER PLATFORM] format — typically 1,000–3,000 words), email newsletter ([YOUR NEWSLETTER PLATFORM] delivers via email to subscribers).

Channel-specific notes: [YOUR NEWSLETTER PLATFORM] content can be cross-promoted on LinkedIn with a short-form text post extracting a key insight and linking to the full piece. The LinkedIn post must deliver standalone value — the [YOUR NEWSLETTER PLATFORM] link is supplementary. [YOUR NEWSLETTER PLATFORM] builds an owned audience outside LinkedIn — if LinkedIn's algorithm changes or reach declines further, the [YOUR NEWSLETTER PLATFORM] subscriber base is unaffected.
