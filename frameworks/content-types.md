# F5a: Content Types

Framework for `content_types` table. Defines content types — the format and packaging of content. A content type answers: "What shape does this take?" Consumed by Ghost Writer (format selection) and Content Distributor (platform targeting).

Content types are distinct from content pillar sub-categories (see F5b). A content type is the container. A sub-category is what goes inside. Example: a case study (sub-category) can be packaged as a carousel, a long-form text post, or a blog article (content types).

Video and podcast content types are out of scope — see F5c.

---

## Short-Form Text Post

What it is: A text-based post, typically under 900 characters. One idea, one takeaway. Consumed in a single scroll without expanding. Punchy, direct, conversational.

Best platforms: LinkedIn, X/Twitter.

LinkedIn: The workhorse format. Highest volume, most stable performer. Fits the mobile-first feed where 91% of browsing happens.

X/Twitter: Native format. Character limit enforces brevity. High velocity — short lifespan, high frequency expected.

---

## Long-Form Text Post

What it is: A text-based LinkedIn feed post, typically 900–3,000 characters. Multiple paragraphs with line breaks. Develops a single argument, tells a single story, or walks through a single framework. Lives in the feed — requires "see more" expansion. Not an article or blog post — this is a feed post that runs longer.

Best platforms: LinkedIn.

LinkedIn: Strong dwell time generator when the hook earns the expansion. The first two lines carry the entire post — if the user doesn't click "see more," the content doesn't exist.

---

## Carousel / Document Post

What it is: A multi-page PDF uploaded as a native document. Each page is a slide — typically 5–15 pages. Visual, structured, swipeable.

Best platforms: LinkedIn, Instagram.

LinkedIn: Highest organic reach format. 2–3x more dwell time than single-image or short text posts. Each page swipe is an engagement signal. Only ~1.4% of content uses this format — low competition, high reward.

Instagram: Native carousel format. Up to 20 slides. Algorithm favours multi-image posts over single images.

---

## Infographic

What it is: A single visual asset that communicates data, a process, a comparison, or a concept through design rather than text. One image — vertical orientation for mobile. Information-dense but visually structured.

Best platforms: LinkedIn, Instagram.

LinkedIn: One of the strongest image formats. High save rate — inherently "save-worthy" which is a strong algorithmic signal. Strong for data visualisation, process diagrams, comparison matrices, market landscape maps, framework summaries. Must be readable on mobile without zooming.

Instagram: Strong for educational and brand-building content. Saveable and shareable.

---

## Blog Post / Article

What it is: Written content, typically 800–2,500 words. Develops a topic in depth. The canonical long-form written asset — other content types are derivatives of this. Can be published on a website (SEO-optimised, permanent, searchable, linkable) or natively on LinkedIn using the article or newsletter feature.

Best platforms: Website/blog, LinkedIn (native article/newsletter), email newsletter.

Website: The canonical version. Owned media. Ranks in search. Drives organic traffic over time.

LinkedIn native article: Same content published natively. Lower immediate feed visibility than posts but higher search discoverability and longer lifespan. LinkedIn newsletters build a subscriber base notified directly on publication — bypasses the feed algorithm for subscribers.

LinkedIn post: A post promotes the article but the full content lives elsewhere. The post must deliver standalone value — the link is supplementary, not the point.

Email: Blog posts / articles are the backbone of most email newsletter content.

---

## White Paper / Research Report

What it is: A long-form, data-heavy document, typically 5–30 pages. Presents original research, analysis, frameworks, or recommendations. Often gated (email required to download) for lead generation. PDF format.

Best platforms: Website (gated download), LinkedIn (promoted with key findings), email newsletter, sales collateral.

Website: Lead generation asset. The gate captures contact information. The content establishes authority.

LinkedIn: Not posted in full. Key findings, data points, or a single framework extracted and posted as a carousel, text post, or infographic. Full report linked or offered in comments.

---

## One-Pager / Cheat Sheet

What it is: A single-page document — PDF or image — that condenses a topic, framework, or process into a scannable reference. Designed to be saved, printed, or bookmarked.

Best platforms: LinkedIn, website (downloadable), email newsletter.

LinkedIn: High save rate — inherently "save-worthy." Works as a single image or as page one of a carousel with supporting context on subsequent pages.

---

## Slide Deck / Presentation

What it is: A multi-slide presentation, typically 10–30 slides. More formal than a carousel — includes title slides, section breaks, and deeper content per slide. Originally designed for live presentation but distributable as a standalone asset.

Best platforms: LinkedIn (uploaded as document), SlideShare, website (embedded or downloadable), email.

LinkedIn: Uploaded as a PDF document post. Functions like a carousel but longer. Best for conference talk summaries, quarterly reviews, comprehensive frameworks. Length can work against it on mobile — each slide must earn the next swipe.

---

## Email Newsletter

What it is: Written content delivered directly to a subscriber's inbox on a regular cadence. Typically 300–1,500 words. Curated, personal, direct. Bypasses all platform algorithms — delivery is guaranteed to subscribers.

Best platforms: Email ([YOUR NEWSLETTER PLATFORM], Beehiiv, ConvertKit, Mailchimp), cross-promoted on LinkedIn and X/Twitter.

Context: The only format where distribution is owned, not rented. Subscribers opted in. Open rates and click-through rates are the primary metrics. Newsletter content can be repurposed across all other content types. LinkedIn's native newsletter feature (see Blog Post / Article) is the platform-specific equivalent.

---

## Thread

What it is: A series of connected posts that build an argument or walk through a topic sequentially. Each post stands partially alone but contributes to a larger narrative.

Best platforms: X/Twitter (native format).

X/Twitter: Native thread format. Each tweet can be engaged with independently. Strong for step-by-step explanations, numbered lists, progressive arguments.

LinkedIn: Threads don't exist natively. The equivalent is a long-form text post with numbered sections or a carousel with sequential slides.
