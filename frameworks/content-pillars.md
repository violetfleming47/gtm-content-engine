# Content Pillars

Framework for `content_pillars` table. Defines the thematic categories your content serves. Each pillar answers: "What territory does this content own?"

Your answers in QUESTIONS.md populate the `[YOUR ...]` sections in this framework.

**Consumed by:** Scorer (pillar alignment), Format Selector (format selection), Repetition Monitor (distribution tracking), Researcher (signal categorisation), Content Suggester (gap detection), Reporter (coverage analysis), Advisor (recommendations), Newsletter Curator (edition planning) — 8 agents, the most-referenced framework.

---

## How Pillars Work

Every piece of content maps to one pillar. Pillars define your content strategy — what topics you own, what's adjacent, and what's out of scope. The Scorer uses pillars to classify incoming content. The Repetition Monitor tracks distribution across pillars to prevent over-concentration. The Content Suggester identifies gaps.

Most B2B content operations need 4–8 pillars. Fewer than 4 limits variety. More than 8 creates classification ambiguity.

## Pillar Template

For each pillar, define:

| Field | What It Is |
|-------|-----------|
| `pillar_slug` | URL-safe identifier (e.g. `enterprise-ai-adoption`) |
| `definition` | What this pillar covers — 2-3 sentences |
| `sub_topics` | Topics within scope |
| `adjacent_topics` | Related but borderline — include with clear angle |
| `out_of_scope` | What does NOT belong here |
| `target_percentage` | Target distribution share across your content calendar |

## Tips for Good Pillars

- **Name the territory, not the format.** "Enterprise AI Adoption" is a pillar. "LinkedIn Posts" is a channel.
- **Each pillar should have a distinct audience intent.** If two pillars serve the same reader need, merge them.
- **Pillars don't need equal weight.** Your primary authority pillar might get 30%. A community/culture pillar might get 10%.
- **Review quarterly.** Pillars that consistently under-produce may be too narrow. Pillars that over-produce may need splitting.
